# Limitations and Future Evolution

The current prototype is deterministic, local-first, and designed for
explainability. The main limitations are explicit:

- Battery cranking telemetry is not yet emitted through the live path.
- Battery state-of-charge drift and ignition-state filtering are not used.
- Brake remaining useful life is not calculated because distance and wear rate
  are not persisted for the detector.
- Tire detection has no steady-driving filter and no per-wheel speed signal.
- Thresholds, temperature coefficients, and energy budgets are not fleet-
  calibrated.
- Simulator ground truth is not independent of simulator telemetry formulas.
- The service is not yet deployed through the repository's GCP `iac/` path.
- Stock Indian vehicles may not expose clean battery OCV, direct per-wheel
  pressure, or direct brake-pad wear. Real-vehicle validation therefore needs
  added instrumentation, independent inspections, or a telematics partnership.

## Prioritized next evidence

1. Run a small instrumented Indian-vehicle pilot with direct battery voltage,
   battery temperature, and per-wheel TPMS.
2. Compare brake-energy estimates with measured pad thickness at service events.
3. Add ignition and cranking measurements before claiming battery M2 coverage.
4. Calibrate thresholds by vehicle class, tire type, ambient regime, and duty
   cycle rather than reusing simulator constants.
5. Re-run the evaluator with labels that were not generated by the detector's
   own equations.

## Evolution path

1. Add production signal provenance and calibration metadata.
2. Persist the fields needed for ignition gating, distance-based wear rate, and
   cranking analysis.
3. Validate thresholds against labeled service and inspection outcomes.
4. Add statistical baselines only where deterministic rules cannot meet the
   required false-alert and lead-time targets.
5. Keep deterministic evidence and explanations as the safety-facing contract,
   even if future models assist ranking or narration.
